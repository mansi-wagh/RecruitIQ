# RecruitIQ Behavioral Interview Guide

## Purpose
Provide a standardized behavioral interview guide to help panels evaluate how candidates demonstrate role-relevant competencies. The guide supports AI-assisted question generation while emphasizing evidence-based scoring and human override.

## Scope
Applies to:

- Behavioral interview loops across teams (engineering, data, operations)
- Interviewers using RecruitIQ’s interview question suggestions

AI is used for preparation and draft summaries only.

## Main Content
### 1. Behavioral competencies to assess
Select competencies mapped to the role competency model. Common examples:

- Ownership and accountability
- Communication and collaboration
- Problem-solving under constraints
- Learning agility
- Stakeholder management

For each competency, define:

- what “good” looks like
- what evidence is acceptable

### 2. Structured interview flow
Use a consistent sequence:

- Set context: clarify the competency being evaluated
- Ask for a specific experience:
  - “Tell me about a time…”
  - limit responses to 5–8 minutes
- Probe for evidence:
  - actions taken
  - trade-offs considered
  - outcomes achieved
- Close with learning and reflection:
  - what the candidate learned or changed

### 3. AI-assisted question generation
RecruitIQ may suggest questions based on skill gaps identified during screening.

Interviewers must ensure:

- every question maps to a competency in the rubric
- questions are open-ended and evidence-seeking
- follow-ups are tailored to the candidate’s story

Operational practice:

- If the AI suggests a question that does not map to the competency rubric, replace it.

### 4. Scoring rubric guidance
Score per competency using a shared scale.

Recommended anchors:

- Strong: demonstrated measurable impact, clear decision-making, consistent behavior
- Adequate: evidence present but impact or reasoning unclear
- Weak: vague or missing evidence; cannot explain actions or outcomes

Evidence requirements:

- the candidate describes actions they personally took
- the story includes constraints, decision criteria, and outcomes

### 5. Evidence vs. impression
Avoid scoring based on:

- confidence, presentation style, or seniority cues

Use evidence fields:

- problem statement
- role played
- actions
- trade-offs
- outcomes and metrics

### 6. Human override triggers
Override AI-drafted evaluation summaries when:

- key evidence is omitted
- the candidate adds stronger examples after follow-ups
- the AI interprets behavior as a skill without confirming rubric alignment

When overriding:

- record which evidence was added or corrected

## Best Practices
- Ask one driver question per competency and use 2–3 follow-ups.
- Use consistent timing across candidates.

## Examples
### Example: Ownership competency
- Follow-up probes:
  - “What decision did you make, and why?”
  - “How did you measure success?”
  - “What risk did you manage?”
- Strong evidence outcome:
  - Candidate describes proactive mitigation and measurable improvement.

## Summary
This behavioral guide supports consistent, evidence-based evaluation with AI assisting question preparation and draft summaries. Interviewers retain responsibility for scoring integrity, documentation, and human override.
