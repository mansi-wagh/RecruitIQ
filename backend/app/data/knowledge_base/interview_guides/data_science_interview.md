# RecruitIQ Data Science Interview Guide

## Purpose
Provide a competency-driven data science interview guide for assessing analytical thinking, statistical reasoning, model evaluation, and production readiness. RecruitIQ AI may suggest questions for missing skills, but final evaluation remains human-led.

## Scope
Applies to:

- data science roles (analytics, ML modeling, experimentation)
- panels evaluating end-to-end modeling workflows

## Main Content
### 1. Competency model for data science
Typical competencies:

- problem framing and data understanding
- feature engineering and modeling rationale
- evaluation metrics and experimental design
- bias, robustness, and reliability considerations
- communication and stakeholder impact

### 2. Interview structure
Recommended sections:

- case walkthrough (problem + data + constraints)
- modeling approach and evaluation
- error analysis and iteration
- operationalization and communication

### 3. AI-assisted question selection
RecruitIQ may suggest questions for missing skills.

Allowed:

- select one question per missing skill
- use follow-ups to validate depth

Disallowed:

- using AI to bypass fundamentals
- relying on resume claims without probing

### 4. Question frameworks (examples)
Problem framing:

- “How did you define the target variable and justify it?”

Evaluation:

- “What metrics did you choose, and why? How did you avoid metric overfitting?”

Experimentation:

- “Describe an A/B test you ran. How did you handle sample size and stopping rules?”

Robustness:

- “How do you detect data drift or leakage?”

Communication:

- “Explain a result to a non-technical stakeholder. What did you emphasize?”

### 5. Scoring rubric guidance
Strong evidence includes:

- correct statistical reasoning
- practical trade-offs (bias/variance, interpretability/performance)
- clear evaluation methodology
- documented iteration and learning

### 6. AI confidence and human override
Override AI evaluation when:

- the candidate’s explanation demonstrates stronger competence than predicted
- the AI suggests an irrelevant gap

When uncertainty exists, ask additional questions around ambiguous competencies.

## Best Practices
- Request concrete artifacts: notebooks, reports, dashboards.
- Require reasoning about metrics and validation.

## Examples
### Example: metric disagreement
The candidate explains why a metric changed and how they adapted evaluation after observing unintended optimization.

## Summary
This data science guide provides competency categories, question frameworks, and evidence-based scoring with AI used strictly for preparation and decision-support.
