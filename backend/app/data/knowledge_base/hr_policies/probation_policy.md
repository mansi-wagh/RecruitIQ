# RecruitIQ Probation Policy (New Hire Decision Support)

## Purpose
Establish consistent probation expectations for new hires while clarifying how progress signals are assessed. Although probation is an HR function, RecruitIQ can support structured feedback loops and evidence-based evaluations during early employment.

## Scope
Applies to:

- New hires in engineering, data, product, design, operations
- People managers responsible for probation check-ins
- HR partners coordinating documentation and outcomes

This document is jurisdiction-agnostic and focuses on internal best practices.

## Main Content
### 1. Probation objectives
Probation is designed to:

- Confirm role readiness against competency expectations
- Provide structured feedback and coaching
- Ensure performance and collaboration meet team standards

### 2. Competency framework and evidence
Managers must define probation success criteria aligned to:

- The role’s competency model (technical + behavioral)
- Team operating norms (communication, delivery, ownership)

Evidence should be collected from:

- Completed milestones (projects, tickets, deliverables)
- Peer and stakeholder feedback
- Structured 1:1 notes and retrospectives

### 3. RecruitIQ AI support (decision-support only)
If RecruitIQ provides analytics or drafted summaries for probation reviews, it is limited to:

- Summarizing manager feedback inputs
- Highlighting patterns in objective evidence (e.g., delivery consistency)
- Suggesting development plans based on rubric coverage

Human control requirements:

- Managers must approve any AI-generated summaries
- Final ratings must be based on documented evidence and rubric definitions

### 4. Check-in cadence
Recommended checkpoints:

- End of week 2: onboarding readiness and initial expectations
- Mid-probation: progress review and gap plan
- End of probation: final evaluation and recommendation

### 5. Development plan and escalation
If gaps are identified:

- The manager and employee define an actionable plan:
  - target competencies
  - measurable milestones
  - support resources (mentorship, training)

Escalation triggers:

- recurring delivery misses without mitigation plan
- repeated rubric-inconsistent behaviors
- inability to collaborate effectively with the team

### 6. Outcomes
Typical outcomes:

- Pass and confirm role scope
- Extend probation with a written plan
- Separation or role adjustment (decision requires HR partnership)

Documenting outcomes:

- Use consistent evidence-based rationale
- Keep performance narratives specific and non-speculative

### 7. Fairness and bias mitigation
Probation evaluations should avoid:

- Over-reliance on subjective impressions
- Penalizing normal ramp-up learning without evidence of persistent non-performance

Instead:

- Compare performance to initial goals and rubric expectations
- Use multiple perspectives and documented outcomes

## Best Practices
- Use measurable milestones, not only completion status.
- Maintain a transparent “expectations dashboard” for the employee.

## Examples
### Example: “Code quality” gap detected
- Evidence: review comments showing repeated defects.
- Plan: pair programming for two weeks + targeted testing guidance.
- Outcome: probation extension with clear acceptance criteria.

## Summary
RecruitIQ’s probation approach is structured and evidence-driven. AI may assist with summarization and development planning, but managers and HR retain decision authority with documented, fair evaluation.
