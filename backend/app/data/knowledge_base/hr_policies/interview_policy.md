# RecruitIQ Interview Policy (Decision-Support)

## Purpose
Ensure RecruitIQ interview guidance is used consistently, transparently, and fairly. This policy standardizes panel responsibilities, AI-assisted preparation, interview structure, scoring integrity, and documentation so candidates receive an explainable, evidence-based evaluation.

## Scope
Applies to:

- Recruiters, hiring managers, and interview panel members
- AI-generated interview question suggestions
- Structured interview scoring and evidence recording

RecruitIQ interview guidance is jurisdiction-agnostic and intended to support best-practice evaluation.

## Main Content
### 1. Interview design standards
Every role must have an interview plan aligned to the competency model used for screening.

- Interview loops should include:
  - A structured technical assessment (as needed)
  - A behavioral component focused on role-relevant competencies
  - Calibration checkpoints for the panel

- Panel members must evaluate using agreed rubrics:
  - Competency categories
  - Evidence examples
  - Rating scale definitions

### 2. How AI support may be used
RecruitIQ may generate interview question suggestions based on:

- Missing skills or gaps identified from resume signals
- Company interview guide content retrieved from the knowledge base

Allowed AI usage:

- Use suggested questions to prepare a structured interview
- Use suggested follow-ups to probe for concrete evidence

Disallowed AI usage:

- Using AI output as the only evaluation basis
- Inferring sensitive attributes not explicitly relevant to job competencies

### 3. Question relevance and fairness checks
Before interviews, the interviewer should validate that every question:

- targets a competency in the role competency model
- can be answered with evidence related to work or projects
- does not encourage unsupported personal characteristics

Operational practice:

- If a question does not map to a competency, replace it.

### 4. Structured scoring and evidence capture
Scoring must be anchored to evidence observed during the interview.

- Each panel member should record:
  - Key candidate statements
  - Demonstrated behaviors
  - Artifacts referenced (e.g., GitHub, case study, metrics)
  - Risk flags (if any) with supporting reasoning

- The panel should score separately before discussion.

### 5. AI-assisted interpretation of answers
If RecruitIQ proposes an evaluation summary, interviewers must treat it as a draft.

Human override triggers include:

- Candidate provided credible counter-evidence
- The AI summary omits critical details mentioned by the candidate
- The AI suggested assessment conflicts with the rubric

When overriding:

- Document what was changed and why
- Confirm the final score reflects rubric-defined evidence

### 6. Documentation expectations
After the interview loop, the recruiting team must produce a consistent package:

- Competency-by-competency ratings
- Evidence excerpts supporting the ratings
- Notes describing how the AI suggestions were applied (or not)

## Best Practices
- Use pre-interview question sets and always tailor follow-ups to candidate responses.
- Keep interviewer notes specific and non-speculative.
- Schedule panel calibration when multiple interviewers assess the same competency.

## Examples
### Example: AI suggests missing “SQL optimization”
- AI-generated question: “Describe an indexing strategy you used…”.
- Interviewer conduct:
  - Ask for constraints, before/after metrics, trade-offs.
- Scoring evidence:
  - Candidate mentions query plan analysis and measurable latency improvement.
- Outcome:
  - Accept rubric rating even if resume looked incomplete.

## Summary
RecruitIQ interview policy ensures structured, evidence-based evaluation with AI used for preparation and draft summarization. Human decisions and documented evidence remain the final authority.
