# RecruitIQ Diversity Policy (Talent and AI Fairness)

## Purpose
Establish principles and operational expectations to promote diversity, equity, and inclusion in hiring and employment decisions. This policy also defines how RecruitIQ’s AI features must mitigate bias and support explainability.

## Scope
Applies to:

- Candidate screening, interviewing, and hiring decisions supported by RecruitIQ
- Training, evaluation of AI decision-support outputs
- Interviewer and recruiter responsibilities

This document avoids jurisdiction-specific legal references and remains vendor-neutral.

## Main Content
### 1. Diversity as a competency for teams
RecruitIQ treats diverse hiring as a business enabler:

- stronger problem-solving through varied perspectives
- improved candidate experience
- better alignment with customer and stakeholder diversity

### 2. Recruiting practices
Recruiters and panel members must:

- ensure job descriptions focus on role competencies
- use structured interview rubrics
- evaluate evidence consistently across candidates

Operational definitions:

- “Evidence” means interview observations and role-relevant artifacts.
- “Competency model” means the agreed mapping of required skills and behaviors.

### 3. Bias mitigation in AI decision support
RecruitIQ’s AI must be used as decision support, not automation.

Mandatory checks before AI output influences decisions:

- verify that recommended questions and scoring criteria map to competencies
- review whether the AI explanation cites resume evidence that exists in the candidate record
- confirm that missing-skill gaps are not inferred from irrelevant factors

When bias risk is suspected:

- require additional human assessment
- consider alternative evidence sources (portfolio, work samples, structured assessments)

### 4. Explainability and transparency
RecruitIQ must support explainability by:

- using retrieved knowledge base excerpts as the basis for AI rationales
- presenting clear reasons in recruiter language (skills overlap, evidence quality, interview rubric alignment)

Explainability limits:

- AI must not provide “guarantees” or certainty beyond evidence.

### 5. AI confidence and human review
If RecruitIQ provides a confidence signal:

- low confidence requires deeper human review
- medium confidence requires verification through structured assessments
- high confidence does not eliminate human review

### 6. Data governance and continuous improvement
RecruitIQ teams must regularly evaluate outcomes:

- measure selection distribution by role and level
- monitor model drift and retrieval quality
- refine competency mappings and rubrics based on calibration results

### 7. Candidate experience standards
Candidates should receive:

- consistent communication timelines
- feedback when appropriate and feasible
- respectful, evidence-based interview conduct

## Best Practices
- Train interviewers on rubric scoring to reduce subjective bias.
- Use multiple evidence points before major decisions.

## Examples
### Example: AI suggests rejection due to skill overlap
- Action: recruiter schedules assessment for the core competency rather than rejecting solely on match score.
- Outcome: decision reflects interview evidence.

## Summary
RecruitIQ’s diversity policy requires consistent, competency-based evaluation and bias-aware AI usage. AI outputs are explainable, confidence-scored, and always subject to human review and evidence validation.
