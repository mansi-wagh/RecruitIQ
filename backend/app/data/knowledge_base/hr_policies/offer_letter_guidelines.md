# RecruitIQ Offer Letter Guidelines (Enterprise Hiring)

## Purpose
Provide consistent guidance for drafting and issuing offer letters in a way that supports transparency, clarity, and evidence-based employment decisions. RecruitIQ’s AI may help assemble offer drafts and summarize interview outcomes, but HR and hiring managers remain responsible for final offer content.

## Scope
Applies to:

- HR and hiring managers preparing offer letters
- Recruitment operations supporting offer workflows
- AI-assisted offer drafting and rationale summaries

Jurisdiction-agnostic, vendor-neutral.

## Main Content
### 1. Offer readiness criteria
Before generating an offer:

- completion of required interview loop(s)
- panel decision recorded with rubric evidence
- confirmation of role scope, level, and compensation bands

AI involvement:

- AI may draft structured summaries of interview rationale.
- Humans validate factual accuracy and finalize offer terms.

### 2. Content structure for clarity
Offer letters should include:

- role title and level
- start date expectations
- reporting line and team description
- compensation summary (base and relevant variable components)
- benefits overview (high level, with references to benefit guides)
- remote/hybrid expectations (if applicable)
- employment terms overview (as provided by HR)
- standard acknowledgements and next steps

### 3. Using RecruitIQ AI responsibly in offer drafting
Allowed AI support:

- convert panel notes into a structured “decision rationale” section
- generate a checklist of onboarding steps

Human validation must cover:

- accuracy of role scope, level expectations, and manager name
- consistency with compensation and banding policies
- removal of any unverified statements

### 4. Explaining hiring rationale (without over-claiming)
If including a “why you were selected” section:

- reference the competency model
- cite evidence themes (e.g., execution quality, collaboration behaviors)
- avoid absolute guarantees or future performance predictions

Operational rule:

- Any claim about candidate performance must trace to documented interview evidence.

### 5. AI confidence score interpretation (if displayed)
RecruitIQ should not present AI confidence as certainty.

If the platform provides a confidence signal for internal use:

- do not display it as a decision basis to candidates
- use it internally to ensure extra human verification when confidence is low

### 6. Audit trail and version control
Offer workflows should maintain:

- versioned offer drafts
- approvals recorded (HR + hiring manager)
- record of interview evidence used for the decision

### 7. Candidate communication and next steps
Offer letters should clearly state:

- acceptance timeline
- documentation required before start
- how the candidate can ask questions

## Best Practices
- Keep offer letters readable and consistent across roles.
- Use a structured onboarding checklist after acceptance.

## Examples
### Example: AI-generated rationale includes an unsupported claim
- Fix: HR removes the sentence and replaces it with an evidence-based bullet from panel notes.

## Summary
RecruitIQ offers guidance for creating clear, consistent offer letters. AI may draft structured summaries and checklists, but HR and hiring managers validate content and ensure decisions remain evidence-based and human-led.
