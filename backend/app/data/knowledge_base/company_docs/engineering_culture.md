# RecruitIQ Engineering Culture (How We Work)

## Purpose
Describe RecruitIQ’s engineering culture and the behavioral expectations used in hiring and performance evaluation. This document supports consistent interview probing and helps RecruitIQ AI generate culturally aligned guidance.

## Scope
Applies to:

- Interviewers and hiring panels
- Engineering leadership and recruiting operations

## Main Content
### 1. Core values translated into engineering behaviors
RecruitIQ promotes behaviors that can be observed and scored with evidence:

- Ownership:
  - candidates/engineers take responsibility for outcomes
  - they propose trade-offs and mitigation
- Clarity:
  - they explain decisions, constraints, and risks
  - they document so others can build and maintain
- Collaboration:
  - they work across teams with feedback loops
  - they share context proactively
- Quality:
  - they prioritize tests, review discipline, and operational readiness
- Learning agility:
  - they iterate based on evidence and feedback

### 2. How the culture shows up in work
Operational expectations:

- Use structured planning (milestones and acceptance criteria)
- Keep a decision log (why a choice was made)
- Run lightweight retrospectives and action items
- Build interfaces and APIs that are maintainable

### 3. Hiring implications
Interview questions should probe:

- examples of ownership (problem → decision → outcome)
- clarity in communication (explaining trade-offs)
- collaboration behaviors (mentoring, cross-team alignment)

AI support expectation:

- RecruitIQ may suggest questions aligned to the competency model
- Interviewers validate evidence during the interview and score against rubrics

### 4. Responsible collaboration and inclusive communication
Engineering practices must support inclusion:

- asynchronous updates for distributed work
- accessible documentation and shared artifacts

### 5. Quality and reliability expectations
Engineering culture requires:

- tests for correctness
- observability for production systems
- safe release practices

### 6. Ethical decision-making
Culture also includes responsible AI and data handling behaviors:

- no unapproved data sharing
- transparent decision logs
- contestable AI outputs with human review

## Best Practices
- Ask for measurable outcomes (“what improved, by how much”).
- Prefer stories that show iteration and learning.

## Examples
### Example: Ownership story
- Candidate describes an incident, triages quickly, implements mitigations, and documents follow-up.

## Summary
RecruitIQ engineering culture is made observable through ownership, clarity, collaboration, quality, and learning agility. Recruiters and panels should use evidence-based interviews to validate these behaviors, with AI supporting preparation but not decision authority.
