# RecruitIQ Recruiter Guide: Resume Screening

## Purpose
Provide a consistent, evidence-based process for recruiters to screen resumes using both human review and RecruitIQ AI decision-support outputs. The guide focuses on reducing bias, improving auditability, and ensuring screening decisions align with the competency model.

## Scope
Applies to:

- Recruiters conducting first-pass screening
- Panels reviewing screening notes
- RecruitIQ AI usage for match scoring, skill-gap summaries, and resume parsing outputs

## Main Content
### 1. Screening objectives
The primary goals of resume screening are to:

- confirm baseline role alignment (competency coverage)
- assess evidence quality (what the candidate actually did)
- determine the right next step (interview, assessment, or additional clarification)

Resume screening must not be used to “guess” competencies beyond submitted evidence.

### 2. Competency mapping before reviewing AI outputs
Before using AI recommendations:

- confirm the competency model and skill categories for the role
- ensure the job description matches the expected evaluation areas
- identify “must-have” vs “nice-to-have” competencies

Operational rule:

- AI may help identify coverage, but the competency model defines the standard.

### 3. How recruiters use AI match score and evidence
RecruitIQ may provide:

- **AI Match Score** for role fit
- extracted skill signals and experience themes
- suggested skill gaps for interview probing

Guidelines:

- Use AI match score for prioritization only.
- Review resume sections where evidence is strongest:
  - Projects with scope and outcomes
  - Role descriptions with responsibilities and metrics
  - Technical stacks listed with context

### 4. Interpreting “skill overlap” and “experience fit”
Use a structured approach:

- Skill overlap:
  - confirm the candidate has used the listed skill in a relevant context
  - validate depth with project detail
- Experience fit:
  - years-of-experience is a coarse signal
  - for borderline cases, rely on evidence and structured questions

Decision rule:

- If evidence is unclear, route to a structured assessment or interview probing for that competency.

### 5. Minimum technical assessment routing
RecruitIQ should trigger additional assessment when:

- the candidate has less than the baseline experience threshold for the role
- core competencies are missing or ambiguous in the resume
- AI confidence signal is low

### 6. Bias mitigation and fairness checks
Resume screening must avoid:

- assumptions based on naming, location, or unrelated background markers
- over-weighting of prestige proxies without evidence

Instead:

- treat all candidates consistently via the competency model
- compare evidence quality (scope, decisions, outcomes)

### 7. Documentation requirements
For each screened candidate, record:

- key evidence bullets (from resume)
- how AI suggestions were applied (if used)
- recruiter rationale for next step

If rejecting based on lack of evidence:

- specify which competencies lacked substantiated evidence
- indicate that reapplication is possible with updated evidence

## Best Practices
- Use short, structured notes with evidence citations.
- Prefer competency coverage plus evidence depth over keyword density.
- Calibrate screening standards across recruiters.

## Examples
### Example: AI match score high, but mismatch in evidence
- AI: indicates strong SQL overlap
- Human review: SQL appears only as a mention, no projects
- Action: move to interview with targeted SQL design and query validation questions.

### Example: AI match score medium, evidence strong through projects
- Candidate: fewer listed skills, but project outcomes demonstrate equivalence
- Action: schedule interview and use AI skill-gap suggestions as probes.

## Summary
RecruitIQ resume screening is a human-led, competency model-based process enhanced by AI decision-support. Recruiters prioritize candidates using AI signals but confirm evidence through structured review and route ambiguous cases to assessments or deeper interview probing.
