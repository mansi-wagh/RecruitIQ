# RecruitIQ Recruiter Guide: Candidate Scoring

## Purpose
Define how to score candidates consistently across recruiting stages using a rubric and how to interpret RecruitIQ AI outputs (match score, confidence signal, suggested gaps) without turning them into automatic decisions.

## Scope
Applies to:

- Recruiters and hiring managers responsible for scoring
- Interview panels using evidence-based ratings
- RecruitIQ AI decision-support features

## Main Content
### 1. Scoring system overview
RecruitIQ recommends a rubric-based scoring system where each competency is rated using evidence.

- Inputs:
  - recruiter screen notes
  - interview evidence and notes
  - optional AI summaries for preparation and draft explanations

- Outputs:
  - competency ratings
  - overall recommendation (advance, assess further, or reject)

### 2. Competency-based rating anchors
A common rating scale:

- **Strong**: consistent evidence, clear reasoning, measurable impact
- **Meets**: sufficient evidence, minor gaps or limited metrics
- **Developing**: incomplete evidence, reliance on generic claims, unclear outcomes

Scoring must include evidence statements:

- What did the candidate do?
- What was the context and constraint?
- What decision did they make?
- What outcome occurred?

### 3. Interpreting AI match score
AI Match Score is not a rating; it is a triage signal.

Recommended interpretation:

- High match score:
  - candidate is likely to meet baseline competencies
  - still validate in interviews
- Medium match score:
  - candidate may meet baseline with targeted probing
- Low match score:
  - candidate requires stronger evidence to advance; consider assessment or clarify ambiguities

### 4. Interpreting AI confidence signal
AI confidence is used to drive human effort, not to finalize outcomes.

- Low confidence:
  - require additional evidence (portfolio/work sample or structured assessment)
- Medium confidence:
  - validate with interview probes and rubric anchoring
- High confidence:
  - still score using evidence; ensure the explanation is grounded in retrieved context

### 5. Handling uncertainty and disagreements
If panel members disagree:

- record separate ratings and evidence bullets
- discuss evidence mapping to rubric anchors
- calibrate to ensure the rubric standard is consistent

When AI suggests a skill gap that appears irrelevant:

- treat it as a hypothesis
- adjust interview questions to match real observed needs

### 6. Documentation and audit trail
For each candidate, maintain:

- overall recommendation with evidence summary
- competency-level evidence bullets
- AI interpretation notes:
  - what AI indicated
  - how the panel validated or rejected the signal

### 7. Decision thresholds (process guidance)
Because roles vary, use thresholds as *guidelines*:

- Advance when:
  - must-have competencies are at least “Meets” with evidence
- Assess further when:
  - must-have evidence is incomplete or AI confidence is low
- Reject when:
  - must-have competencies lack evidence and assessments/interviews do not validate capability

## Best Practices
- Do not average raw AI values into final decisions.
- Require evidence bullets for “Strong” ratings.
- Train panels to score using the same rubric anchors.

## Examples
### Example: AI confidence low but interview evidence strong
- AI indicates missing “distributed retries” evidence
- Candidate answers with concrete implementation and mitigation
- Outcome: score based on interview evidence; override AI skill-gap assumption.

## Summary
Candidate scoring in RecruitIQ is rubric-driven and evidence-based. AI outputs guide triage and question planning. Final scoring requires structured evidence and panel calibration, with documentation for any overrides.
