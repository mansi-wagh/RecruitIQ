# RecruitIQ Recruiter Guide: Interview Question Bank (Competency-Aligned)

## Purpose
Provide a structured, competency-aligned question bank approach that recruiters and interviewers can use with RecruitIQ AI suggestions. The goal is to ensure questions elicit evidence, map to the competency model, and remain fair and explainable.

## Scope
Applies to:

- Recruiters and panel interviewers
- Technical and behavioral interviews
- RecruitIQ AI-generated question suggestions

## Main Content
### 1. Question bank design principles
A good question bank should:

- map directly to competencies in the role model
- be scenario-based when possible
- ask for actions, reasoning, trade-offs, and outcomes
- avoid questions that require private attributes unrelated to job performance

Operational definition:

- **Evidence-seeking question**: a question that can be answered with concrete examples and measurable outcomes.

### 2. Competency templates
Use templates to ensure consistent evidence capture.

#### Template A: Experience + trade-off
- “Describe a time you faced X constraint. What options did you consider, and why did you choose one? What was the outcome?”

#### Template B: Troubleshooting
- “Walk us through how you diagnosed a problem, what signals you checked, and how you validated the fix.”

#### Template C: Design reasoning
- “Design a solution for Y. Explain what you optimized for, what you considered, and how you ensured correctness.”

### 3. AI-assisted selection workflow
RecruitIQ may suggest questions based on missing skills.

Process:

- select one question per missing competency (or per critical competency)
- add 2–3 follow-ups based on candidate answers
- ensure follow-ups still map to the rubric

Override rule:

- if AI suggests a question that does not map to the competency or cannot be answered based on role context, replace it.

### 4. Question examples by interview type
#### Behavioral
- Ownership:
  - “Tell me about a time you owned a problem end-to-end. What decisions did you make, and what impact resulted?”

- Collaboration:
  - “Describe a situation where stakeholders disagreed. How did you drive alignment?”

- Learning agility:
  - “Give an example of how you ramped up quickly. What was your learning plan and what changed after feedback?”

#### Backend (examples)
- API design:
  - “How would you model pagination and filtering, and what failure modes do you consider?”

- Reliability:
  - “Explain idempotency and retry strategy. How do you prevent duplicate side effects?”

- SQL correctness:
  - “Describe how you validate a query for correctness and performance.”

#### Frontend (examples)
- State management:
  - “How do you prevent unnecessary re-renders in complex UIs?”

- Accessibility:
  - “Walk through how you test accessibility for keyboard and screen-reader users.”

### 5. Best practices for follow-ups
Follow-ups should be evidence-specific:

- scope: “What was your exact responsibility?”
- constraints: “What limitations existed?”
- validation: “How did you verify correctness?”
- outcomes: “What improved, by how much?”

## Best Practices
- Keep the number of questions manageable (depth beats breadth).
- Calibrate question difficulty to role level.
- Record evidence notes using a consistent format.

## Examples
### Example: Using a competency question effectively
- Original question: “Describe a design trade-off you made.”
- Follow-ups:
  - “What were the alternatives?”
  - “How did you measure success?”
  - “What would you do differently now?”

## Summary
RecruitIQ’s interview question bank approach uses competency templates and evidence-seeking structures. RecruitIQ AI assists in selection, but interviewers remain responsible for rubric alignment and human-led evidence scoring.
