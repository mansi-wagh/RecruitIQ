# RecruitIQ Recruiter Guide: Rejection Guidelines (Evidence-Based)

## Purpose
Provide guidelines for issuing candidate rejection decisions in a way that is respectful, evidence-based, and consistent. This includes how RecruitIQ AI recommendations can inform rejections but must not automate them.

## Scope
Applies to:

- Recruiters and hiring managers
- Panels preparing decision rationales
- AI decision-support outputs used during rejection workflows

## Main Content
### 1. Principles of respectful rejection
Rejection decisions must be:

- evidence-based (tied to competencies and observed gaps)
- transparent in a non-legal, non-jurisdictional way
- respectful in tone

RecruitIQ expects decision support to:

- avoid over-claiming causality
- avoid implying AI made the decision

### 2. When AI may influence rejection
RecruitIQ AI may provide:

- low match score signals
- confidence signals
- identified missing skills

Rejection can be considered when:

- must-have competencies lack evidence after interview/assessment
- the panel confirms through structured scoring that expectations are not met

### 3. When to override AI and avoid premature rejection
Override AI-based pessimism when:

- the candidate provides credible equivalent evidence
- evidence was missing due to résumé phrasing or limited artifacts
- the AI identified an irrelevant skill gap

In these cases:

- consider additional probing questions
- consider a practical assessment

### 4. Evidence documentation requirements
Before final rejection, document:

- which competencies were not met
- what evidence was considered (and why it was insufficient)
- the stage where the gap was validated (resume, interview, assessment)

### 5. Candidate communication approach
Communication guidelines:

- provide a concise, respectful message
- focus on role competency fit rather than personal attributes
- offer constructive next-step suggestions when feasible

Avoid:

- disclosing sensitive internal scoring details
- implying that AI alone determined the outcome

### 6. Offer future opportunities
Where appropriate:

- invite candidates to reapply after they improve relevant evidence
- recommend resources consistent with the role competency model

### 7. Examples of evidence-based rejection reasons
Use competency-based language:

- “Insufficient evidence of competency in X despite structured probing.”
- “The required experience for Y was not substantiated with project outcomes.”

## Best Practices
- Use consistent templates across roles.
- Ensure the rejection reason matches the competency model.
- Provide feedback that can help candidates improve.

## Examples
### Example: Missing must-have evidence
- Reason: candidate did not demonstrate reliable evidence of API design and data correctness.
- Action: send feedback that emphasizes portfolio/work sample focus.

### Example: AI suggested gap, interview clarified
- AI indicated missing SQL optimization.
- Interview revealed equivalent optimization experience.
- Outcome: do not reject due to AI-only signal; revise decision path.

## Summary
RecruitIQ rejection guidelines emphasize evidence-based, competency-aligned decisions. AI recommendations can support the analysis, but rejection must be human-led with documented competency outcomes and respectful communication.
